Tuesday · July 28, 2026

Minneapolis, tomorrow evening

A shortlist for one weeknight in the city. Two things are actually on the calendar that night; the rest is the reliable stuff that's open on a Tuesday.

  • Sunset ~8:44 PM — a long, usable evening
  • It's a Tuesday — no cover charges, no waits, most kitchens close by 10
  • Downtown is walkable — Target Field and Target Center are three blocks apart

🕗 Times and lineups drift. Tap through to the venue before you drive anywhere — every card below links to the source.

On the calendar that night

Both downtown, both done by ~10.

6:40 PM

Twins vs. Royals

Target Field · North Loop

A midweek July ballgame is the cheapest good evening in Minneapolis — weeknight seats go for less than a movie, and the gates open two hours early if you want to eat inside. Sun's on the third-base side until late.

Lynx at Target Center

Target Center · Downtown

Outside, while it's light

You have until 8:44. Use it.

6–9 PM

Stone Arch Bridge & Mill Ruins

Mill District · free

The postcard walk: across the bridge, down through the ruins, back along West River Parkway. Twenty minutes if you're efficient, an hour if you're not. Best light is the last half hour before sunset.

Mill Ruins Park →
6–9 PM

Minnehaha Falls

Longfellow · free

Fifty-three feet of waterfall inside city limits, plus the stairs down to the creek if you want to earn it. Sea Salt Eatery is right at the top for a basket of fish tacos in the park.

Minnehaha Park →
Golden hour

Chain of Lakes

Bde Maka Ska / Lake Harriet · free

Rent a canoe or a surrey at Bde Maka Ska, or just do the Harriet loop and end at the bandshell — there's usually free music on the summer schedule, so check what's on before you pick a lake.

Till dark

Sculpture Garden & Loring Park

Loring · free

The Walker's garden is open late and costs nothing — Spoonbridge and Cherry, then over the Irene Hixon Whitney bridge into Loring Park. The most reliable no-plan plan in the city.

Music & comedy

Tuesday lineups get posted late — check the night of.

Doors ~7

First Avenue & 7th St Entry

Downtown

The Entry — the small room off the side of the Mainroom — is where the weeknight shows live. Cheap tickets, unknown bands, black walls, stars on the outside you'll want to read on the way in.

Icehouse & Berlin

Eat Street / North Loop

Two rooms that treat a Tuesday like a real night: Icehouse for jazz and whatever the local scene is doing this month, Berlin for a candlelit set you'll want a table for.

Acme Comedy Co.

North Loop

One of the best-run comedy clubs in the country, hidden in a basement in the warehouse district. Weeknight shows are one set, reservations only, and the dinner downstairs is genuinely fine.

Matt's Bar vs. the 5-8 Club

A cheeseburger with the cheese sealed inside, invented on this street, claimed by both bars on it. You cannot resolve this from a webpage. Order one at each, a mile apart, and decide.

Before 8

Eat Street or the Global Market

Nicollet Ave · Midtown

Nicollet south of downtown is a long row of Vietnamese, Ethiopian, Mexican and Greek in a few blocks. Midtown Global Market is the indoor version — good, cheap, and it closes early, so go before the evening plan, not after.

North Loop patios

Washington Ave

The obvious pick if you're already downtown for a game — brick warehouses, patios on every block, and a ten-minute walk from either arena. Young Joni's back bar if you want the version with a door you have to find.

Two restored neighborhood movie houses running second-run films and repertory nights for less than a matinee downtown. The Riverview's 1950s lobby is worth the ticket by itself.

Mill District

Even without a ticket, the Endless Bridge and the amber-windowed lobby are open to walk into, and the view down the river is the best one in the city from indoors. Check what's on stage first — it's often worth the seat.
